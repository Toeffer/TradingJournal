### BC8.DE — Bechtle AG — the strongest new lead, and why it still fails

This is the one new name whose *thesis* changed materially this week, and the 18:07Z run
mischaracterised its information state — it rejected BC8.DE as "five days to earnings" with "July
preliminary beat and guidance upgrade already known."

In fact, on **2026-11-05 — three days before this run** — Bechtle issued an **ad-hoc release** with
preliminary Q3 figures: business volume up about **19% to roughly EUR2.44bn**, earnings before tax
up about **14% to roughly EUR92m**, and a **raised 2026 EBT growth forecast of above 10%**, with
final figures to follow on **2026-11-13**. That inverts the risk description: the price-sensitive
information is already public under ad-hoc disclosure rules, so Nov 13 is substantially a
confirmation event rather than the binary it was described as, and the name would sit naturally in
the `post-earnings-drift` family rather than being excluded as a pre-earnings gamble.

It is nonetheless **REJECT** this run, for reasons that are specific and not about the thesis:

1. **Verification gate.** The ad-hoc release is corroborated across several secondary outlets but
   `bechtle.com` is unreachable, so this run could not open the issuer source. `secondary_only`
   cannot enter Actionable or Early Watch.
2. **No price data covering the event.** The repository EU history ends 2026-11-03, two sessions
   *before* the ad-hoc release. There is literally no observable price reaction to analyse, so no
   gap, no consolidation high, no stop and no drift target can be defined. A `post-earnings-drift`
   setup requires the gap to hold above its midpoint — unmeasurable here.
3. **Liquidity is thin.** 20-session ADV is **EUR5.55m/day** (159,395 shares), only just above the
   EUR5m non-US preferred floor, on a EUR34 share. Spread and slippage would matter at the EUR150
   position size.
4. **Data integrity.** The Nov 3 row for BC8.DE reads open 35.580 against high 34.340 — the open is
   above the session high, which is impossible. See Operational findings.

Red team: **REJECT**, but on evidence access and measurability rather than merit. Promotion path for

sessions, and assess the post-release structure as a drift candidate with a real stop. Pre-mortem:

Nov 13 confirmation sells the news.

## Operational findings

Three repository-level problems surfaced while doing the work. They affect future runs more than
this one.

1. **All primary-source hosts are blocked in the scheduled environment.** Detailed above. Until the
   research domains are allowlisted, every scheduled run is structurally incapable of producing a
   valid Actionable candidate, and will keep bottoming out at NO NEW TRADE for a reason unrelated to
   the market. This is the single highest-value fix.
2. **The EU scanner is effectively blind.** Its last run was `scan-eu-2026-11-03-0039.md` at 00:39
   CET on Nov 3 — *before* the Nov 3 XETRA session — so it could not see the TUI1.DE breakout that
   session produced, and it has not run in the five days since. US scans have run daily over the
   same period (Nov 3, 4, 5, 6, 7). EU scans over the last two months are sporadic: Sep 22, Oct 6,
   Oct 13, Oct 16, Nov 3. The consequence is concrete: the only genuine breakout in this week's
   universe was found by screening `eu_quote_history.csv` by hand, not by the scanner, and it would
   have been missed entirely by a scanner-first run. The canonical snapshot has carried **zero EU
   rows** for two consecutive weeks as a result.
3. **`eu_quote_history.csv` has a stale-open defect on 2026-11-03.** BC8.DE records open 35.580 with
   high 34.340, and KGX.DE records open 34.060 with high 33.940 — in both cases the open appears
   carried from the prior session and exceeds that session's high. Any setup whose stop or trigger
   depends on an opening price from these rows would be built on bad data. Worth a check of the EU
   quote writer before these rows are used for levels.

4. **`archive_recommendation_reviews.py` archives by date, not by run.** `output_schema.md` states
   that an archive "contains exactly that run's review rows", but the script selects every review row
   whose `reviewed_at` date matches `--date`. On this second same-day run it therefore wrote four rows
   — the 18:07Z run's two plus this run's two — into `decisions-2026-11-08-2143.csv`. The earlier
   `decisions-2026-11-08.csv` is untouched and the ledger still validates, so nothing is corrupted, but
   the two same-day archives overlap and the second is a superset rather than a per-run slice. The
   script also stamps the suffix from its own clock rather than the run timestamp; the archive pair was
   renamed from `-2150` to `-2143` so that report, manifest and decision archive share one run suffix
   (content and SHA-256 unchanged, re-verified after renaming). Worth either a `--run-at` argument or
   per-run tagging in the review ledger.
